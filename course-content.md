# Basic Filming For Social Media — Course Structure

## Course Tagline
You don't need a $5,000 camera. You need a plan.

## Modules (8 lessons + 3 quizzes)

---

### Lesson 1 — Your Phone Is Enough
**Key message:** You already have everything you need to start.
- Modern smartphones shoot 4K. The camera in your pocket is genuinely better than professional cameras from 10 years ago.
- What actually matters: lighting, framing, and audio — not the camera.
- Recommended (not required) gear: a tripod ($20–$40) and a clip-on lavalier mic ($15–$30).
- Avoid the "I'll start when I have better gear" trap — your first 50 videos are practice anyway.

### Lesson 2 — Setting Up Your Shot
**Key message:** Framing makes or breaks a video before you say a word.
- Rule of thirds: put your eyes on the upper third of the frame.
- Portrait mode (9:16) is the native format for Reels and TikTok — always shoot vertical.
- Distance: arm's length for talking-head; wider for demonstrations.
- Background: clean, uncluttered, or intentionally gym-branded. Avoid busy backgrounds.
- Lighting: face a window or use a ring light. Never shoot with a bright window behind you.
- Eye line: camera at eye level or slightly above — never below (unflattering and unprofessional).

### Lesson 3 — The Danger Zone (Safe Zones for Reels & TikTok)
**Key message:** The edges of your screen are covered by platform UI — keep critical content out of there.
- On Instagram Reels and TikTok, the outer edges are overlaid with controls: like buttons, share icons, captions, profile handles.
- Anything in those zones will be hidden or hard to read.
- The **Edits app by Meta** (free) shows you the exact safe zone overlay so you can frame within the limits before you post.
- Rule of thumb: keep all text, faces, and key visuals in the centre 80% of the frame.
- Same applies to on-screen text and captions you add in editing.

### Lesson 4 — The First 3 Seconds & The Millennial Pause
**Key message:** You have 3 seconds. Don't waste them.
- The algorithm judges your video by how many people watch past the 3-second mark. If they scroll, it stops distributing.
- Your hook must be visual AND verbal — show something interesting AND say something compelling immediately.
- Hook formulas: "Stop doing X", "Here's why your [thing] isn't working", "Watch this before you [action]".
- **The Millennial Pause:** The awkward 1–2 second silence before you start talking when you hit record. It kills your hook.
  - Fix: start talking BEFORE you hit record, then trim the beginning in editing.
  - Or use a countdown cue and start mid-sentence.
- Edit out all dead air at the start and end of every clip.

### Lesson 5 — Getting Good Audio
**Key message:** Bad audio kills good video. People will forgive grainy footage — they won't forgive bad sound.
- Built-in phone mic is OK outdoors in calm conditions; indoors it picks up echo and room noise.
- A clip-on lavalier mic ($15–$30) is the single best upgrade you can make.
- Record in a small, soft room (clothes in a wardrobe absorb echo brilliantly).
- Avoid filming near HVAC vents, fans, or open windows with traffic.
- Check your audio levels before every recording — speak at normal volume and watch the meter.
- In editing: use noise reduction to clean up background hum. Most free apps (CapCut, Edits) have this built in.

### Lesson 6 — How Long Should Your Videos Be?
**Key message:** Long enough to deliver value. Short enough to hold attention.
- Reels sweet spot: **15–30 seconds** for pure hook/entertainment content; **45–90 seconds** for educational content.
- TikTok: similar — 30–60 seconds performs best for most gym content.
- Don't pad. Every second that doesn't add value loses viewers.
- Watch time percentage matters more than total watch time — a 20-second video watched to completion beats a 2-minute video watched halfway.
- **Trial Reels on Instagram:** Instagram lets you post a Reel as a "Trial" — it's shown to non-followers first. If it performs well, Instagram promotes it to your existing audience too. Use this for experimental content you're not sure about — it won't tank your existing engagement if it flops.

### Lesson 7 — The Goal of Reel Content & Why Education Wins
**Key message:** Reels bring in new eyes. Education builds trust. Trust sells memberships.
- Reels are a **discovery tool** — they are shown to people who don't follow you yet.
- Unlike Stories (for existing followers) or feed posts (for engagement), Reels are designed to reach new audiences.
- **Educational content** is the highest-performing content type for gym owners because:
  - It positions you as the expert (not just someone who works out).
  - It provides value before asking for anything.
  - It's shareable — people send "how to" content to friends.
  - It attracts people who are already interested in fitness, meaning they're pre-qualified leads.
- Content ideas: form tips, nutrition myths busted, "why you're not seeing results", workout modifications, gym etiquette.
- The goal isn't to go viral — it's to reach the right 500 people in your city.

### Lesson 8 — Captions, Auto-Translation & Getting Found
**Key message:** Captions are not optional. But translation needs a strategy.
- **85% of social media videos are watched without sound.** Captions are not a nice-to-have — they're essential.
- Both Instagram and TikTok offer auto-captions. Turn them on. Review them for errors before posting.
- Captions also help with accessibility and keep viewers engaged longer.
- **Auto-translation:** Instagram and TikTok can auto-translate your captions into other languages to reach a global audience.
  - **Recommendation: Turn this OFF** if your gym is location-based.
  - More views from people who will never visit your gym doesn't help you. You want local views, not global vanity metrics.
  - Exception: if you sell online coaching or digital products, translation can help.
- **Hashtags:** Use 3–5 specific hashtags. Avoid mega-hashtags (#fitness has 500M posts — you'll be buried). Use local + niche tags (#[yourcity]gym, #crossfitcoach, #hiitworkout).
- **Posting time:** Post when your audience is active — typically 6–9am or 6–9pm local time.
